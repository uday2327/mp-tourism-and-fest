Underrated Arts of Madhya Pradesh

Patangarh, Dindori

Gond Painting

Gond painting is a traditional tribal art of central India, especially Madhya Pradesh, created by the Gond community. Its history comes from ancient wall and floor paintings made during festivals and rituals. Artists use dots, lines, and bright natural colors to show animals, trees, and daily life. Each painting often tells a story connected to nature and folklore. Its significance lies in preserving tribal culture and expressing a deep bond with nature.

Jhabua and Bhopal

Bhil Painting

Bhil painting is a tribal art form made by the Bhil tribe of Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan. It is very old—its roots go back hundreds of years in tribal wall paintings. Artists use bright colors and dot patterns to depict nature, animals, and folklore. Traditionally it was done on house walls during festivals and rituals. Its significance is in preserving Bhil culture and showing their close connection to nature.

Malwa region

Mandana Art

Mandana art is a traditional folk art from Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan made by rural women. It is very ancient, believed to be practiced for hundreds of years during festivals and rituals. Designs are drawn on floors and walls using white chalk or lime on red mud backgrounds. Common motifs include peacocks, flowers, animals, and geometric patterns. Its significance is in welcoming prosperity, protecting the home, and preserving folk traditions.

Alirajpur

Pithora Painting

Pithora painting is a sacred tribal art of the Rathwa and Bhilala communities of Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat. It is centuries old and traditionally painted on house walls during rituals. The paintings are made to honor Baba Pithora, a tribal deity, seeking blessings and prosperity. Artists use natural colors to draw horses, humans, and nature scenes. Its significance lies in its religious value and preservation of tribal culture.

Homes and courtyards

Chowk Poorana

Chowk Poorana (Chowk Purana) is a traditional floor art practiced in Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh. It is an ancient folk art, followed for hundreds of years during festivals and rituals. Women draw designs on the floor using rice paste, flour, or natural colors. Patterns include flowers, footprints, and geometric shapes. Its significance is to welcome gods, bring good luck, and decorate homes on auspicious occasions.

Bagh, Dhar district

Bagh Print

Bagh printing is a hand block textile art using natural dyes and red-black motifs, developed along the Bagh river.Bagh Print is a traditional hand block printing art from Bagh village in Madhya Pradesh. It is several centuries old and uses natural dyes and wooden blocks for printing. Artisans print mainly on cotton and silk with floral and geometric designs. The craft is known for its rich red and black colors made from natural sources. Its significance is in preserving eco-friendly textile traditions and local heritage.

Tarapur, Neemuch

Nandna Block Print

Nandna print is a Bhil-influenced block printing tradition with signature motifs like mirch, champakali, amba, and jalam buta.Nandna Block Print is a traditional textile craft from Nandana village in Madhya Pradesh. It is an old hand block printing tradition practiced for generations. Artisans use carved wooden blocks and natural dyes to print on cotton fabric. Designs often include floral and geometric patterns in earthy colors. Its significance lies in preserving handmade textile heritage and eco-friendly printing.

Chanderi, Ashoknagar

Chanderi Weaving

Chanderi fabric is prized for its light, sheer texture and gold zari motifs,woven in the historic town of Chanderi.Chanderi weaving is a famous handloom textile tradition from Chanderi in Madhya Pradesh. It is several centuries old, with roots going back to royal patronage in medieval times. Chanderi sarees are known for their lightweight fabric, shine, and fine motifs. Weavers use silk, cotton, and zari to create elegant designs. Its significance lies in its royal heritage and preservation of India’s handloom culture.

Maheshwar

Maheshwari Weaving

Maheshwari textiles were developed in Maheshwar under Ahilyabai Holkar and are known for striped or checkered bodies with zari borders.Maheshwari weaving is a traditional handloom textile art from Maheshwar in Madhya Pradesh. It began in the 18th century under the patronage of Queen Ahilyabai Holkar. Maheshwari sarees are known for light weight, soft texture, and striped borders. Weavers use silk and cotton to make elegant, reversible designs. Its significance lies in royal heritage and sustaining local handloom traditions.

Bherugarh, Ujjain

Batik Print

Bherugarh is a hub for batik, where hot wax resist and dyeing create the classic crackle effect on fabric.Batik print is a fabric art technique where melted wax is applied to cloth to make patterns before dyeing. It is a very old art, more than 1,000 years old, and became popular in India as a textile craft. After dyeing, the wax is removed to reveal beautiful designs. Common motifs include flowers, leaves, and traditional patterns. Its significance is in its unique handmade style and cultural artistic value.

Old Bhopal

Zari Zardozi

Zari zardozi embroidery survives in the lanes of Bhopal, using metallic threads and beads to build ornate motifs.Zari Zardozi is a traditional Indian embroidery done with gold and silver metallic threads. It is centuries old and was promoted during the Mughal period in royal courts. Artisans decorate fabrics with zari, beads, and sequins to create rich designs. It is commonly used on wedding wear, sherwanis, and festive clothing. Its significance lies in its royal look and preservation of luxury hand embroidery traditions.

Datia and Tikamgarh

Bell Metal Ware

Datia and Tikamgarh are known for bell metal ware made with lost-wax casting, producing detailed ritual and decorative objects.Bell Metal Ware is a traditional metal craft from Tikamgarh in Madhya Pradesh. It is an old craft where artisans make utensils and decorative items from bell metal (copper + tin alloy). This craft has been practiced for generations by skilled metalworkers. Items like lamps, plates, and idols are commonly made. Its significance lies in preserving traditional metal artistry and cultural heritage.

Betul district

Bhareva Shilp (Dhokra)

Bhareva Shilp is the local Dhokra metal craft of Betul, shaped by lost-wax casting into lamps, figurines, and ritual items.Bhareva Shilp is a traditional metal craft of Betul district in Madhya Pradesh. It is a form of Dhokra metalwork where artisans use lost-wax casting to create decorative and functional items. The craft involves shaping metal using wax models, which are then melted away to leave behind hollow metal objects. Items like lamps, figurines, and ritual objects are commonly made. Its significance lies in preserving ancient metalworking traditions and cultural heritage.

Mandla to Balaghat

Bamboo Basketry

Bamboo craft flourishes in southern Madhya Pradesh, where tribal artisans weave baskets, trays, and utility items with coiled and wicker techniques.Bamboo Basketry is a traditional craft where baskets and utility items are made from bamboo. It is very old and has been practiced in Indian villages for centuries. Artisans weave bamboo strips to make baskets, mats, and storage items. The craft uses natural, eco-friendly materials and simple tools. Its significance lies in sustainable living and preserving tribal and rural traditions

Bundelkhand

Lipai (Chitera Wall Painting)

A Bundelkhand wall art tradition where Chitera painters create linear patterns on mud-plastered surfaces using fingers and natural pigments.Lipai (Chitera Wall Painting) is a traditional folk wall art from the Bundelkhand region of Madhya Pradesh. It is very old and begins with coating walls using mud and cow dung (called lipai). Women then paint designs with natural colors and sometimes add mirrors. Common motifs include flowers, birds, and geometric shapes. Its significance is in home decoration, rituals, and preserving rural traditions.

Bundelkhand

Bundelkhand Terracotta

Traditional terracotta figures and animal forms made by local potters, often placed at shrines and village spaces. Bundelkhand Terracotta is a traditional clay craft from the Bundelkhand region of Madhya Pradesh. It is an ancient art, practiced for hundreds of years using natural clay. Artisans shape and bake clay to make idols, toys, and decorative items. Designs often reflect village life, animals, and folk beliefs. Its significance lies in preserving rural craftsmanship and cultural heritage.

Bundelkhand

Clay Toys & Ceramics

Decorative clay toys and ceramics are a well-known craft in Bundelkhand, shaped with local clays and hand finishing.Bundelkhand Clay Toys & Ceramics are traditional crafts from the Bundelkhand region of Madhya Pradesh. They are centuries old and made using local natural clay. Artisans shape and fire clay to create toys, pots, and decorative items. Common themes include animals, birds, and village life. Their significance lies in preserving folk art and supporting rural artisans.

Orchha (Bundelkhand)

Orchha Pottery & Basket Weaving

In Orchha’s Ganj area, visitors can see potters and basket weavers at work, keeping Bundelkhand’s everyday crafts alive.Orchha Pottery & Basket Weaving are traditional crafts from Orchha in Madhya Pradesh. These crafts are centuries old and use local clay and natural grasses. Pottery includes pots and decorative items, while baskets are woven for daily use. Designs are simple and inspired by village life and nature. Their significance lies in supporting local artisans and preserving folk traditions.

Culture & Famous Dances (Region-wise)

A quick look at Madhya Pradesh’s folk traditions by area and place.

Malwa (Indore, Ujjain, Dewas): The lively Matki dance is performed by women balancing pots, while Gair is a vibrant group dance popular during festivals.

Nimar (Khargone, Barwani, Khandwa): The colorful Bhagoria dance is tied to fairs and harvest celebrations, featuring energetic group movements.

Bundelkhand (Orchha, Tikamgarh, Chhatarpur): The graceful Rai dance is performed with expressive footwork and is a signature of Bundelkhand folk culture.

Baghelkhand (Rewa, Satna, Sidhi): The storytelling Gaur Maria and Ger forms reflect tribal traditions and community gatherings.

Gond & Baiga Regions (Mandla, Dindori, Balaghat): Gond tribal dances celebrate nature, seasons, and village rituals, often performed in groups.

Central MP (Bhopal, Sehore, Raisen): Jhumar and Phag dances are performed during spring and Holi celebrations.

Chhindwara & Betul: Suwa (Parrot) dance is performed by tribal women, celebrating community life and harvest.
